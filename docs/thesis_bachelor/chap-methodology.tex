\section{Methodological Approach}

The project was carried out as an iterative software engineering project. The business problem was known from the start: Buy2Sell wanted to reduce manual registration work by scanning product labels and extracting useful registration data. The technical solution was uncertain. The team had to determine which fields could be extracted safely, how model output should be controlled, and how the application should behave when extraction or submission failed.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth,height=0.70\textheight,keepaspectratio]{artifacts/unified-process-model.png}
  \caption{Unified Process model showing time boxes and the change of focus over time.}
  \label{fig:unified-process}

The project therefore used a Unified Process-inspired structure combined with a lightweight sprint workflow. Unified Process was used to organize the work around recurring disciplines such as requirements, design, implementation, validation, and documentation. The sprint workflow was used to turn those disciplines into short implementation increments with concrete deliverables.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{p{3.2cm} p{5.2cm} p{5.2cm}}
\hline
\textbf{Method element} & \textbf{Use in the project} & \textbf{Reason} \\
\hline
Unified Process & Used as an overall planning model with overlapping disciplines. & Requirements, design, implementation, and validation had to evolve together. \\
Sprint workflow & Used to divide the work into short increments. & The prototype needed frequent integration and adjustment as technical risks became visible. \\
Risk-driven development & High-risk workflow boundaries were implemented early. & Capture, extraction, validation, review, and submission had to work together. \\
Human-in-the-loop design & AI output was treated as draft data requiring user review. & Product labels may be incomplete, ambiguous, damaged, or visually unclear. \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{Methodological approach used in the project.}
\end{table}

\section{Project Phases}

The work was divided into five practical phases. The phases were not strict handover points. Later implementation work still affected requirements and design, but the phase structure made the work easier to plan, implement, and document.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{p{2.8cm} p{4.4cm} p{4.4cm} p{3cm}}
\hline
\textbf{Phase} & \textbf{Main question} & \textbf{Work performed} & \textbf{Output} \\
\hline
Discovery & What problem should the prototype solve? & Analysed the project brief, meeting notes, label fields, and workflow constraints. & Scope, field list, initial requirements. \\
Foundation & Can the app support the basic workflow? & Created Expo project structure, routing, local storage, and capture/import flow. & Running app skeleton with persisted attempts. \\
Extraction & Can label evidence become structured data? & Added barcode detection, provider-based vision-language extraction, prompt contract, parsing, and schema validation. & Draft structured JSON from label images. \\
Review and ingest & Can the user safely accept and send data? & Added Confirm and Edit screen, accepted payloads, HTTP submission, idempotency, and offline queue. & Reviewed payload submission flow. \\
Hardening & Is the prototype reliable enough to demonstrate? & Added settings, diagnostics, retry behavior, automated tests, and documentation. & Testable prototype and report evidence. \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{Project phases and outputs.}
\end{table}

\section{Key Engineering Decisions}

Several decisions were made during development because of project constraints, technology constraints, and workflow risks.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{p{3.4cm} p{4cm} p{4.2cm} p{2.8cm}}
\hline
\textbf{Decision} & \textbf{Alternatives considered} & \textbf{Reason} & \textbf{Consequence} \\
\hline
Use Expo managed workflow & Native React Native, custom native modules. & Faster cross-platform development and access to camera, image picker, SQLite, and secure storage. & Local VLM execution was excluded from the prototype. \\
Use VLM-first extraction & OCR-only pipeline, OCR plus rules, OCR plus LLM. & The system needed field mapping, not only text transcription. & Provider output had to be validated and reviewed. \\
Keep barcode decoding separate & Let the VLM read barcodes from the image. & Barcode values are deterministic evidence when decoded successfully. & Barcodes are merged conservatively into the result. \\
Require human confirmation & Fully automatic submission. & Some fields are ambiguous or require operator judgement. & Submission only happens after user acceptance. \\
Use local queueing & Fail submission immediately on network error. & Workshop network and endpoint availability may vary. & Accepted payloads can be replayed later. \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{Key engineering decisions and consequences.}
\end{table}

\section{Technology Selection}

Technology choices were made according to the prototype goals: cross-platform mobile support, camera access, local persistence, configurable remote extraction, and a reviewable JSON submission flow.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{p{3.2cm} p{4.2cm} p{5.8cm}}
\hline
\textbf{Area} & \textbf{Selected technology} & \textbf{Reason} \\
\hline
Mobile runtime & Expo and React Native & Cross-platform mobile implementation with web support and managed access to device features. \\
Routing & Expo Router & File-based navigation that matched the application screens. \\
Language & TypeScript & Static checking for structured data and service boundaries. \\
Persistence & Expo SQLite and Drizzle & Local storage for attempts, settings, queue items, and typed database access. \\
Validation & Zod & Runtime validation of model output and internal payloads. \\
AI integration & AI SDK provider adapters & Common abstraction for provider-based vision-language extraction. \\
Testing & Jest and React Native Testing Library & Automated validation of services, repositories, utilities, and selected UI behavior. \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{Technology choices and rationale.}
\end{table}

The choice of Expo also introduced limitations. The managed workflow supports portability and fast development, but it makes custom native model runtimes difficult. For that reason, local vision-language inference was treated as future work rather than part of the prototype.

\section{Architecture and Design Method}

The architecture was developed around separation of responsibilities. Route files were kept thin, screen modules handled user-facing workflow state, services coordinated business processes, repositories isolated persistence, and API modules handled provider and ingest communication.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{p{3.2cm} p{5.2cm} p{5.2cm}}
\hline
\textbf{Layer or module type} & \textbf{Responsibility} & \textbf{Reason} \\
\hline
Routes & Map URLs and tabs to screens. & Keeps Expo Router files small and navigation-focused. \\
Screens & Render workflow state and collect user decisions. & Keeps UI behavior separate from persistence and integration logic. \\
Services & Coordinate capture, extraction, submission, and queue replay. & Makes workflow behavior testable outside the UI. \\
Repositories & Read and write attempts, queue items, and settings. & Isolates persistence details from business logic. \\
API modules & Handle provider calls, proxy routes, and ingest transport. & Keeps external communication behind explicit boundaries. \\
Types and schemas & Define structured item and payload contracts. & Makes model output and internal data validation consistent. \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{Architecture responsibility model.}
\end{table}

The most important design principle was that AI output should not become authoritative without review. Barcode decoding, schema validation, deterministic merge rules, diagnostics, and human confirmation were therefore used as control points around the probabilistic extraction step.

\section{Validation Strategy}

Validation was divided into implementation validation, acceptance validation, and KPI evaluation.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{p{3.6cm} p{5.2cm} p{5cm}}
\hline
\textbf{Validation level} & \textbf{Purpose} & \textbf{Evidence} \\
\hline
Implementation validation & Check technical behavior of services, repositories, schemas, retries, and queueing. & Automated Jest tests, type checking, linting. \\
Acceptance validation & Check that the operator can complete the capture-review-send workflow. & Manual end-to-end walkthroughs. \\
KPI evaluation & Measure extraction accuracy and define separate timing targets. & Extraction accuracy requires a fixed label dataset and reviewed ground truth. Time reduction requires a separate timed operator comparison. \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{Validation levels used in the project.}
\end{table}

Automated tests validate the implemented workflow under expected and failure conditions. They do not by themselves prove extraction accuracy. Extraction accuracy must be measured against a fixed dataset of real label images with reviewed ground truth. Registration time reduction is a separate timing question because it depends on operator behavior, correction speed, and manual-entry baseline measurements. It therefore remains defined as a timed evaluation target rather than being derived from the field-correctness artifact.

Generative AI was used as a development aid for selected implementation, testing, refactoring, and documentation tasks. Generated output was not treated as authoritative. Code and text produced with AI assistance were reviewed, tested, and adjusted before inclusion.

\section{Method Limitations}

The methodology was suitable for building a working software prototype, but it does not by itself prove production effect. The project validates technical feasibility: image capture, barcode enrichment, provider-based extraction, schema validation, human review, submission, and offline queueing. Full business validation requires empirical KPI evaluation with real labels, ground truth data, timed manual baselines, and production-build measurements.
